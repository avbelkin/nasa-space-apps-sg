# Architecture & Harmonization Design Notes

This repository implements the [Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots](./README.md) solution as a decoupled static SPA and containerized API.

## System Split

### 1. Frontend (`ui/` — Pure React + TypeScript + Vite on GitHub Pages)

- **Public Availability**: Publicly accessible on GitHub Pages at [https://avbelkin.github.io/nasa-space-apps-sg](https://avbelkin.github.io/nasa-space-apps-sg), deployed via [deploy-ui.yml](../.github/workflows/deploy-ui.yml).
- **Earth-First Mapping Engine (`maplibre-gl`)**: Keyless **MapLibre GL JS** canvas defaulting to a **3D `globe` projection** (with an optional 2D `mercator` toggle) over **EOX Sentinel-2 Cloudless (`s2cloudless-2025_3857`)** raster WMTS imagery (`{z}/{y}/{x}.jpg` with required EOX/Copernicus attribution) to visualize active-fire hotspots, typical seasonal activity bands, and current calendar anomaly departures directly on Earth.
- **Two-Tier CSS Grid Layout**:
  - **Outer Controls Wrapper (`.app-frame`)**: CSS Grid page shell wrapping perimeter controls *around* the map (stacked below 60rem):
    - `topbar` holds the **Earth Fire Calendar** title and a short description of the map.
    - `sidepanel` holds the All sensors, MODIS, and VIIRS filter with the pinned 24-hour detection chart directly below it. The chart header identifies the selection and shows its coordinates and fire radiative power. Unpin or press Escape to close it.
    - The side panel groups live 24-hour NASA FIRMS metrics, the map legend, and data-source credits at the bottom. The legend reflects visible layers and shows the map coordinates and zoom. The footer shows brief source credits while **Sources and credits** is collapsed and full credits when expanded.
  - **Inner Map CSS Grid Container (`.map-stage`)**: The full-bleed MapLibre canvas spans all tracks under two HUD areas: `hud-top-end` holds a column of icon buttons (zoom, My Location with its error message beside it, reset view), a `2D | 3D` segment, the orbits, clouds, rain clouds (`z6+`), night-time lights and live fire spots toggles and, while spots pulse, a pause-pulse toggle (folded into two columns on maps narrower than 36rem or shorter than 34rem, and four columns below 26rem), paired with the top-end rain time lookback pill (`-30m` / `+30m` steppers + preset selector when rain clouds are visible above `z6` or a lookback offset is active); `hud-bottom-end` (the map column, beside the control column so the two never stack on a short map) holds the satellite overpass freshness card while orbits are shown (a foldable card: open by default on maps at least 48rem wide, folded to its "Satellite passes" header on narrower maps so it does not hide marks at the default view). Map credits appear in the side panel under **Sources and credits**.
- **Sensor filtering and accessibility**. The All sensors, MODIS, and VIIRS controls filter live fire detections, satellite tracks, and the satellite pass card. The controls support keyboard navigation, touch targets, and light, dark, and forced-color themes. Fire spot size reflects FRP.
- **URL Hash, LocalStorage & Geolocation State**: Viewport (`z`, `lat`, `lng`) and controls (`sensor`, `proj`, `orbits`, `clouds`, `rain` (only `rain=0` when off), `rainBack` (30-min snapped lookback up to `720`, omitted at `0`), `lights` (only `lights=0` when off), `fires` (only `fires=0` when off), plus extensible feature parameters; one codec registry in `urlHashState.ts`) sync continuously into `window.location.hash`, and the same state into `window.localStorage` (`nasa_fire_calendar_state_v2`; the older `nasa_fire_calendar_url_hash` key, auto-written by earlier builds, is ignored). A URL hash wins; otherwise viewport, sensor, projection, orbits, clouds, rain, rainBack, lights, and fires restore from storage. Legacy `month` and `region` parameters are ignored. Orbits, clouds, rain clouds, night-time lights and fire spots are on by default. The default viewport opens on Singapore (`1.3521° N, 103.8198° E`) at 3D globe zoom `z3`. On a first visit (no URL hash or saved `localStorage` state), browser geolocation runs once in the background: it marks the visitor's position and only rotates the globe (keeping `z3`) when the located coordinates are at least `100 km` from Singapore, preventing camera bounce for nearby visitors; the HUD **My Location** button explicitly jumps and zooms to the user's coarse browser position, capped at default max zoom `z7`.

Toggled from the map control column (`clouds` hash key).
- **Smooth Global Rain Clouds Layer (`rainCloudLayer.ts`, `rainCloudShader.ts`)**: Renders keyless `256×256` NASA GIBS WMTS `IMERG_Precipitation_Rate_30min` (GPM half-hourly) precipitation tiles fetched exclusively at `z3` (covering tiles below `z3` subdivided into `z3` quads to prevent 3D globe horizon gaps), indexed through the embedded GPM palette into `304×304` wide-gutter (`24px`) `gl.RG16F` half-float textures pre-filtered with 3 separable Gaussian passes and smoothly sampled in WebGL2 with a 9-point isotropic Cubic B-Spline filter. Projects a map-anchored, zoom-octave nested (`L_0 ⊂ L_1`) staggered hex dot pattern whose opacity scales `0 → 1` from the experimental `0.30` cutoff (`RAIN_DOT_INTENSITY_CUTOFF`) to strong rain cores (`0.72+`) in bright blue (`#4d96ff` in experimental `solid` mode, with `rainbow` and `white` modes kept switchable) composited over an easily removable neutral overcast cloud backdrop (`ENABLE_RAIN_CLOUD_BACKDROP` / `setCloudBackdropEnabled`). Blends in linearly across `z6` → `z9` as VIS/IR clouds fade out, holds full opacity `z9` → `z11`, fades out `z11` → `z14.5`, supports 30-minute lookback stepping (`Latest`, `-1h`, `-2h`, `-4h`, `-6h`, `-12h`, resolved against GIBS `WMTSCapabilities.xml` `Domains`), and is stacked directly below `satellite-swaths-wide-fill`. Toggled from the map control column (`rain` / `rainBack` hash keys).
- **Night-Hemisphere VIIRS Black Marble Layer (`nightLightsLayer.ts`)**: Renders keyless `256×256` NASA GIBS Gap-Filled BRDF-Corrected Day/Night Band tiles (`VIIRS_NOAA20_GapFilled_BRDF_Corrected_DayNightBand_Radiance`) via a MapLibre GL JS WebGL2 `CustomLayerInterface` masked in real time to Earth's nighttime side of the solar terminator across `z0–z4`, smoothly cross-fading across `z4` → `z6` into EOX Sentinel-2 Cloudless directly underneath `satelliteCloudLayer.ts`. Toggled from the map control column (`lights` hash key, `lights=0` when off).
- **Live Animated Active-Fire Spots (`fireSpots.ts`, `fireSpotsAnimation.ts`, `fireTimeline.ts`, `satelliteOverpassTimeline.ts`, `FireDetectionsTimelineCard.tsx`)**: Streams compact 24h NASA FIRMS detections (`MODIS_NRT` + `VIIRS_NOAA20_NRT` + `VIIRS_NOAA21_NRT`) every 15 minutes from `/api/fires/spots`, applying hierarchical `z1`–`z7` spatial decluttering above `z1` and rendering a crisp core circle layer scaled by FRP (`1–250 MW -> 0.5–1.5x`) and shaded by confidence (`25–100% -> 0.6–1.0`) above 4 spatial phase-cohort pulse layers (`80ms` tick modulated by 24h linear activity decay `[1 -> 0]` and `sin(2π * cycles + phase)` frequency sweep). Automatically pauses pulsing after 5 minutes of user inactivity, on hidden tabs, when scrolled out of the viewport, or when `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is enabled while preserving static spot legibility, and supports declutter-aware 8 px click selection, expanding hidden `z1`–`z6` neighbors merged into a visible leader spot. Pinned details (`1+` detections) show a 24h (`24 × 1h`) timeline plotted on the browser's local clock with a place-relative smooth `sin(alt)` solar horizon curve over a horizon-split background (`#1e293b` above horizon, `#000000` below horizon), confidence-opacity satellite-colored FRP bars (`>= 1rem` slot columns using the shared Terra/Aqua/NOAA-20/NOAA-21 palette matching the legend and orbital tracks, grouping multiple same-hour spacecraft as equal-width side-by-side sub-bars under `<winner> +N` rotated labels), client-side J2 orbital **fire negatives** (propagated over `-26h..+30m` from server-supplied live CelesTrak TLE lines with a `0.05°` spatial cell cache and `±25m` cross-slot positive-pass suppression, rendered on diagonal-striped grey columns), a right-side NRT **processing-time cutoff** zone (`90m` VIIRS / `120m` MODIS) with muted pending-pass indicators, and a visually hidden semantic data table. Toggled from the map control column (`fires` hash key, `fires=0` when off).
- **Live MODIS & VIIRS Orbital & Swath Overlay**: Renders a two-part `-180m` past ground-track tail per satellite (solid ready tail older than each satellite's NRT processing lag — `90m` VIIRS / `120m` MODIS — and dashed `4,2` in-processing tail within the lag window up to the live spacecraft position, split seamlessly across the `±180°` antimeridian, with a `+30m` forward propagation buffer in the API payload for smooth `30fps` client-side interpolation between refreshes, and no future ground-track line rendered), heading-locked dual instantaneous sensor scan swaths in `extremum` palette styling (wide swath `#ffc078` dashed `4,6` at `0.0875` fill / `0.32` stroke below nadir swath `#92b4de` at `0.14` fill / `0.32` stroke; `400 km` nadir and `2,330 km` MODIS / `3,040 km` VIIRS wide swaths), rotated spacecraft SVG badges alongside nadir position markers, and a "Satellite passes" viewport overpass freshness card (latest pass per satellite, `ready` / `processing`, `~Xh delay`, `Next: <Sat> in ~Xm`) that refreshes on debounced map center moves (`≥ 25 km`) and every minute while visible with live elapsed-minute progression independent of `prefers-reduced-motion`, animated smoothly at `30fps` along the fetched CelesTrak orbital window. Picking a satellite flies to where it is at that moment.
- **Social Link Preview & Canonical Metadata**: Configures Open Graph (`og:*`) and X/Twitter (`summary_large_image`) tags in `ui/index.html` backed by a `1200×630` hero preview thumbnail (`ui/public/og-preview.png`) with build-time `VITE_SITE_URL` resolution in `ui/vite.config.ts` so deployed GitHub Pages unfurls use absolute HTTPS URLs without NASA branding.

### 2. Backend (`backend/` — ASP.NET Core `.NET 10` Container on Google Cloud Run)

- **CelesTrak TLE Caching, Swath Geometry & Overpass Freshness (`Satellites/`)**: Downloads TLE catalogs from CelesTrak (`GROUP=resource` for MODIS Terra/Aqua; `GROUP=weather` for VIIRS NOAA-20/NOAA-21), caches them for 12 hours in memory with embedded fallback TLEs, propagates J2 secular LEO trajectories, builds `49`-vertex (`12`-segment edge) high-latitude/polar-clamped nadir and wide swath polygons (`SatelliteSwathBuilder`), and evaluates 10-day lookback / 24-hour lookahead viewport overpass freshness (`SatelliteOverpassEvaluator`).
- **FIRMS Ingestion & Caching Proxy (`Firms/`)**: Directly queries the official NASA FIRMS REST Area API (`api/area/csv/{MAP_KEY}/{source}/{world|bbox}/{days}`) across `MODIS_NRT`, `VIIRS_NOAA20_NRT`, and `VIIRS_NOAA21_NRT`, normalizing satellite labels (`Terra`, `Aqua`, `Suomi NPP`, `NOAA-20`, `NOAA-21`) and MODIS/VIIRS confidence values (`0–100`; `low`/`nominal`/`high` mapped to `25`/`65`/`95`), caching area snapshots in `IMemoryCache` for 20 minutes, slicing to a rolling 24h window ("as of now", avoiding UTC calendar-day gaps) with `WindowStartUtc`/`WindowEndUtc` bounds and a 2-minute stale fallback if upstream refreshes fail, response compression, and server-side `FIRMS_MAP_KEY` isolation.
- **Spatiotemporal Harmonization Pipeline**:
  1. **Spatial Gridding**: Maps point detections onto a uniform grid (cell size ≥ 1 km, e.g. fixed equal-area / H3 / degree grid) while accounting for MODIS/VIIRS `scan` × `track` footprint growth toward scan edges.
  2. **Temporal Binning & Deduplication**: Aggregates detections per grid cell within daily/pentad windows to eliminate artificial inflation from multiple same-day satellite overpasses (Terra, Aqua, Suomi NPP, NOAA-20, NOAA-21).
  3. **Cross-Sensor Calibration**: Uses the post-2012 MODIS–VIIRS overlap period to align active-fire cell density and Fire Radiative Power (`FRP`) quantiles into a continuous multi-decade metric.
- **Satellite Cloud Proxy (`Clouds/`)**: Proxies `https://layers.extremum.org/v3/weather/satellite/{z}/{x}/{y}.png` (`z` `1..7`) with explicit `Referer`, `User-Agent`, and per-instance `gis-uuid` cookie plus `IMemoryCache` and in-flight request deduplication so client globes avoid upstream rate limits.
- **REST API Endpoints**:
  - `GET /health` — Cloud Run readiness/liveness probe.
  - `GET /api/status` — Service metadata and environment diagnostics.
  - `GET /api/fires/spots` — Compact rolling 24h (configurable `hours=1..48`, `sensor=harmonized|modis|viirs`, `bbox=w,s,e,n` or `world`) active-fire spots feed queried directly from NASA FIRMS Area API (`api/area/csv`) and cached for 20 minutes (`MODIS_NRT`, `VIIRS_NOAA20_NRT`, `VIIRS_NOAA21_NRT`).
  - `GET /api/fires/spots.geojson` — GeoJSON FeatureCollection projection of the cached rolling active-fire Area API snapshot.
  - `POST /api/fires/spots/geometry` — Geometry for up to 512 pinned detections. Send a `spots` array of live row identities (`lng`, `lat`, `timestampMs`, and `satellite`). The response returns the batch status and, when ready, geometry in request order. An unmatched detection has a null entry.
  - `GET /api/satellites/orbits` — Time-bound orbital ground tracks, nadir position, heading, speed, swath parameters, TLE lines (`tleLine1`/`tleLine2` for client-side 24h negative-pass evaluation), computed `swaths` (`nadir`/`wide`), and `satelliteHud` overpass freshness entries (optional `lat`/`lng` query parameters) for Terra, Aqua, NOAA-20, and NOAA-21.
  - `GET /api/weather/satellite/{z}/{x}/{y}.png` — Cached `258×258` live `VIS/IR` satellite cloud tile proxy backed by `layers.extremum.org`.
  - `GET /api/anomalies/summary` *(planned)* — Regional & AOI current situation summary compared against historical calendar baselines (`fitsTypical`, `anomalyScore`, `zScore`, percentile).
  - `GET /api/seasonal/profile` *(planned)* — Harmonized multi-decade monthly/pentad climatology vs. current-year trajectory for the bottom seasonal scrubber and side inspector.
  - `GET /api/hotspots` — FIRMS area detections (`bbox=w,s,e,n`, `days=1..5`, `sensor=all|modis|viirs`).

#### Deferred hotspot geometry

Loading a FIRMS snapshot queues one enrichment job per cached snapshot. A bounded worker reuses the existing [CelesTrak](https://celestrak.org/) TLE cache and shares orbit samples across detections. The default responses omit observation geometry.

The app loads `/api/fires/spots` immediately and follows up with `enrichGeometry=true`, sharing the deferred refresh with land cover. On the follow-up, `geometryStatus` reports `pending`, `unavailable`, or `ready`. A ready response appends `geometry` to `columns` and the geometry object or null to each spot tuple. Existing indexes are preserved. Pinned Observation Details reads those fields from the feed and uses the pinned lookup for missing rows.

The Live Fires status groups data-service connectivity, detections loaded, geometry results and pending counts, land-cover status, and weather counts. It shows when the last check succeeded. While work is pending and the tab is visible, the app checks every ten seconds while counts move, then backs off to thirty seconds and finally one minute when they stay unchanged. A failed check keeps the previous counts and retries. Weather reports both targets checked and targets with at least one available value. Targets outside the bulk limit are listed separately. A completed batch can still contain null fields.

Add `includeGeometry=true` to any of the three hotspot endpoints. The response can wait up to one second for the worker and includes a top-level `geometry` object:

- `geometry.status` is `pending` while work is queued or running (including when the queue is full), `unavailable` if the batch failed and is backing off, or `ready` on completion. Fetch the same query again to check progress. The base detections are returned in all cases.
- When ready, `geometry.spots[i]` describes `spots[i]`, `hotspots[i]`, or `features[i]` in **that response**. Results are kept in memory per instance for up to 20 minutes. A refreshed snapshot or a different instance can return pending again. With request-based Cloud Run billing, background work can pause when the instance is idle; opted-in requests give it time to progress without a deployment configuration change.
- `orbit` reports slant range in kilometers, spacecraft off-nadir angle (from the Earth-center direction), and the hotspot's view zenith and azimuth, with TLE epoch and source. The existing J2 orbit is sampled every 15 seconds from 1 minute before through 7 minutes after the FIRMS time to estimate the pixel's cross-track observation at closest approach. The window accounts for minute precision and the [MODIS/VIIRS granule duration](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-04/MCDWD_VCDWD_UserGuide_RevE_04.22.25.pdf). A missing orbit, TLE more than five days from acquisition, or implausible swath match yields `orbit: null`; Sun and Moon data then use the reported FIRMS time. `estimatedObservationUtc` records the time used.
- `sun` and `moon` give signed geometric elevation above the local horizon and azimuth clockwise from true north. `relativeAzimuthDeg` is the ground-relative azimuth separation (0°–180°). `sensorLookSeparationDeg` is the angle at the satellite between its look toward the hotspot and its direction toward the light source. `glintAngleDeg` is the angle from the ideal flat-surface specular reflection to the hotspot-to-satellite ray: smaller values mean closer alignment. Glint is null below the horizon or without a plausible orbit. The definition is used by the [MODIS Collection 6 fire paper](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/imported/Giglio2016_C6_Fire.pdf) (§3.6.1) and the [VIIRS 375 m fire algorithm](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) (§2.3.4); applying it to the Moon describes geometry only, with no fire reclassification.
- `moonPhaseAngleDeg` runs from 0° (full) to 180° (new). `moonIlluminatedFraction` runs from 0 to 1 and `moonDistanceKm` is measured from the hotspot to the Moon's center. The positions use [USNO solar coordinates](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/sun_approx) and leading terms from [Van Flandern and Pulkkinen's lunar ephemeris](https://astroserver.ftmc.uam.es/faae-sea_curso_calculo_astronomico/presentaciones/pdf/FAAE2A1.pdf). Like the phase and angle fields in [NASA Black Marble](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/products/VJ146A1), these describe lighting conditions; calibrated irradiance would also require a phase-dependent light model. The estimates ignore terrain, refraction, cloud and atmospheric effects, and cannot replace the instruments' per-pixel geolocation.

## Hotspot land cover

The backend samples the [2019 Copernicus C3S land cover map v2.1.1](https://doi.org/10.24381/cds.006f2c9a), which continues the ESA Climate Change Initiative Land Cover series. [Microsoft Planetary Computer](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/dataset/esa-cci-lc) hosts the 32 Cloud Optimized GeoTIFFs. Worldwide requests sample their categorical 1.2 km overview. Regional requests use the native 300 m grid. Both use the same source class codes.

Land cover is a fixed 2019 reference. A circle with the same area as the FIRMS scan × track pixel approximates the acquisition footprint. If either dimension is missing or invalid, sampling uses the published nominal sensor radius. Class shares are estimated across the circle at the reported raster resolution, including across tile boundaries and the antimeridian. A world overview pixel can contain an entire VIIRS footprint, in which case the result represents one coarse class.

All three hotspot routes return cached land cover with their normal response. When `landCoverPending` is true, the caller can repeat the request with `enrichLandCover=true` to fill more of the cache. The web app starts this after the fire feed loads and makes up to three deferred calls per refresh while visible. Each API instance runs at most one pass at a time and starts up to four source requests per second, with at most 160 source requests or 42 seconds of raster I/O per pass. It reads just the blocks intersecting spots and reuses compressed blocks for nearby detections, along with cached footprint summaries. The cache is local to each API instance and warms independently.

- The live compact payload appends `landCover` to `columns` and each spot tuple. Existing fields retain their indexes.
- Raw area hotspots expose `hotspots[].landCover`. Live GeoJSON exposes `features[].properties.landCover`.
- A populated object reports `dominantCode`, `coverage` (the fraction of the footprint with classified data), and `classes` (code/fraction pairs sorted by descending share, with ties resolved by the lower code). Class shares use only classified area. `landCoverDataset` identifies the year, sampling resolution and class labels.
- `landCover` is null for unclassified footprints and for footprints awaiting required blocks. FIRMS detections remain available.

## Live hotspot weather enrichment

The [24-hour weather enrichment proposal](./live-hotspot-weather-enrichment.md) ranks weather, surface, and sensor fields for comparing recent MODIS and VIIRS detections. It describes how to attach available weather at each spot's acquisition time.

The app starts weather sampling on its deferred fire-feed refresh with `enrichWeather=true`. The initial `GET /api/fires/spots` reports cached progress without scheduling weather work. The deferred request queues up to 64 unsampled locations and acquisition times from the 512 most recent unique locations and times in its rolling 24-hour selection. A bounded worker groups nearby points by forecast hour and tile, and caches samples across overlapping FIRMS snapshots. The response reports `weather.status` (`pending`, `ready`, or `partial`) and counts for sampled, pending, available, targeted, and eligible locations. A sampled location can still have unavailable fields.

This pass warms GFS model grids, GWIS fire-weather rasters, NOAA CPC soil anomaly GeoTIFFs, and WFAS fuel moisture GeoTIFFs. Pinned weather requests reuse those samples and fetch the remaining fields through the existing sources. The deferred feed returns progress counts. Full weather values are available through the selected-spots route. The sample cache is bounded and local to each Cloud Run instance.

Radar and the visible/IR mosaic still use point queries. The nowCOAST coverage service advertised no raster layers for those products during verification. NOAA's separate MRMS distribution serves numeric reflectivity as 16-bit PNG-packed GRIB2 frames suitable for scene batching. Using that feed would require routing by native coverage and processing large frames within a bounded memory budget. Calibrated GOES observations, observed lightning, supplemental forecasts and air quality, and global standardized drought stay on the selected-spots path in this pass. [NOAA MRMS numeric products](https://www.nco.ncep.noaa.gov/pmb/changes/MRMS_SCN_v12.1_Upgrade_Supplemental.pdf)

`POST /api/fires/spots/weather` takes up to 16 live spot identities in the same format as the geometry request. The response's `spots` entries follow request order. An identity outside the rolling 24-hour FIRMS snapshot has a null entry. Successful entries include the FIRMS acquisition UTC timestamp and the source, model, sampled grid point, units, valid interval, and age at acquisition for their weather values.

The primary forecast sources use the Extremum reference backend's approach of selecting the run and valid time explicitly and sharing decoded grids across requests for nearby points. The weather endpoint groups hotspots by NOAA model run, valid hour, product, and 4° tile. Each tile is fetched and decoded once per batch and reused from cache. The sampler uses bilinear interpolation for continuous fields and selects the nearest cell for precipitation flags. The response includes `runUtc`, `publishedAtUtc`, and the field's validity interval. It verifies the GRIB run and valid hour against the requested file and uses the NOAA public mirror's object modification time to establish that the file was available by acquisition. [NOAA public GFS inventory](https://registry.opendata.aws/noaa-gfs-bdp-pds/) · [NOAA GFS product inventory](https://www.nco.ncep.noaa.gov/pmb/products/gfs/)

The supplemental hourly forecast and air-quality series each use one multi-location request per batch, with duplicate locations removed. They include weather codes, precipitation accumulations, and aerosol and pollutant concentrations. Precipitation totals cover the latest complete 3- or 24-hour window ending on or before acquisition. A missing hour makes the total unavailable. The supplemental forecast and daily fire-weather series report validity but do not establish the forecast publication time.

The daily fire-weather source supplies Fire Weather Index, danger class (1–7), Fine Fuel Moisture Code, Drought Code, and modeled lightning flash density where covered. `validDateUtc` identifies the forecast date selected by the spot's acquisition date. These daily products carry date-level precision. A transparent coverage pixel yields `no_source_coverage`. The Fine Fuel Moisture Code and Drought Code describe fire weather and are distinct from dead-fuel moisture percentages and soil-moisture anomalies. [Copernicus GWIS fire danger](https://gwis.jrc.ec.europa.eu/about-gwis/technical-background/fire-danger-forecast)

Observed radar reflectivity and satellite imagery samples use NOAA's published frame catalogue. Each sample uses the newest advertised frame at or before acquisition, within eight minutes for radar or one hour for the hourly satellite mosaic. Radar is regional. The satellite mosaic reports native visible and longwave infrared counts within its advertised latitude coverage. Calibrated temperature and cloud classifications require a product with calibration and quality metadata. [NOAA GMGSI product](https://www.ospo.noaa.gov/products/imagery/gmgsi/)

NOAA model subsets include surface and near-surface temperature, dew point, humidity, visibility, winds, irradiance, cloud cover, cloud-layer base and top pressures, estimated heights above mean sea level, cloud ceiling, column water, precipitation-type flags, snow depth and cover, and modeled soil water. Cloud-height estimates use log-pressure interpolation within the model's geopotential-height profile. Plant-available water is weighted by layer thickness at 0–40 and 0–100 cm relative to the model's field capacity and wilting point. For the same depths the backend reports the modeled amount of water needed to reach field capacity in mm, with zero at or above capacity. The sampler retains native averaging windows.

Cloud liquid water and cloud ice water are separate mixing ratios in kg/kg on the 925, 850, 700, 500, and 300 hPa levels. Values require a model level above the sampled surface. [NOAA GFS field inventory](https://www.nco.ncep.noaa.gov/pmb/products/gfs/gfs.t00z.pgrb2.0p25.f003.shtml)

Within GOES East or West coverage, native ABI observations provide calibrated 10.3 μm brightness temperature, cloud class, estimated cloud fraction with valid-sample coverage over the approximate hotspot footprint, and a quality-filtered smoke flag. The same sources supply good-quality cloud optical depth at 640 nm, cloud-top geopotential height in meters, and cloud-top phase. Each raster retains its completed scan times. GLM reports good-quality observed flash starts within 50 km during the latest three contiguous, completed scans. The GLM output carries actual window bounds. [NOAA GOES-R data products](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/our-satellites/currently-flying/goes-east-west/goes-r-series-data-products)

Copernicus GDO soil-moisture anomalies use the latest 10-day or end-of-month period completed at acquisition. The backend reports the native standard deviation from the 1995–2023 climatology and its dry-to-wet class. This indicator represents the upper two LISFLOOD soil layers. [European Commission JRC GDO soil-moisture anomaly](https://data.jrc.ec.europa.eu/dataset/4ce9599e-b3e1-406d-a87b-b98b814565da)

Over its North American coverage, NOAA CPC supplies weekly NLDAS Noah soil-moisture departures in mm. The backend samples 0–10, 10–40, and 40–100 cm, then adds the available layers to obtain 0–40 and 0–100 cm. A total requires all its component layers. Weekly values carry the source's published validity dates and are available once that week has ended at the spot's acquisition time. The upstream analysis generally lags by about four days. [NOAA CPC weekly soil-moisture profile](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/Drought/Monitoring/weekly-SM-noah.shtml) · [NLDAS drought methodology](https://www.drought.gov/data-maps-tools/north-american-land-data-assimilation-system-nldas)

The US Forest Service WFAS feed supplies daily 10-, 100-, and 1,000-hour dead-fuel moisture in percent over the contiguous United States. The rotating layers identify yesterday's observations and today's and tomorrow's forecasts. The sampler resolves those offsets against the raster's publication date in US Mountain Time and matches the acquisition on the same calendar. It uses the observation summary when available, or a forecast issued by acquisition time. The response includes the source calendar date and time zone in `validDateLocal` and `validDateTimeZone`, the raster revision time in `publishedAtUtc`, and whether the value is observed or forecast. The 10-hour raster reports the daily minimum. These daily values have no verified hour of validity.
