# Weather enrichment for the 24-hour fire feed

Earth Fire Calendar displays NASA FIRMS detections from a rolling 24-hour window. Use the current FIRMS snapshot and frames available from the live weather source. Attach weather and surface context to each reported spot using its acquisition time in UTC. Comparing recent detections can help explain why MODIS and VIIRS report different spots or fire radiative power (FRP) over the same area.

The [same-family baseline and retraining follow-up](./architecture-and-harmonization.md#same-family-second-pass-baseline) and [cross-family baseline](./architecture-and-harmonization.md#cross-family-second-pass-baseline) provide reference scores for evaluating enriched features on missed second detections.

Start with cloud along the viewing path, view angle, pixel footprint, and thermal contrast against the local background. These can affect whether a sensor sees a fire. Wind, recent rain, and fuel moisture help explain changes in the fire and its plume between passes. [MODIS fire detection algorithm](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6110110/) · [VIIRS 375 m algorithm](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) · [MODIS and VIIRS detection limits](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/19/10123/2026/)

## Weather fields by priority

The priority reflects the value of testing a field for cross-sensor differences. Field availability and coverage depend on the weather source and model. Derived values should preserve the definitions and units of their inputs.

| Priority | Weather layers | Per-spot values and use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Worth checking | Weather satellite visible and infrared imagery, including combined radar/satellite views | Source scene UTC time, cloud mask or probability, cloud fraction over the fire footprint, calibrated infrared brightness temperature in K, and a smoke flag where available. Use an acquisition-matched mask when available. A combined view can share the same inputs as the separate imagery and radar layers. |
| Worth checking | Total, low, middle, and high clouds, cloud base and top, fog, visibility | Total and layer cloud fractions, cloud base and top in m with their height references, fog or rime code, and horizontal visibility in m. Cloud layers overlap. Sample total cover independently. Horizontal visibility describes conditions near the ground. |
| Worth checking | Weather radar, rain and thunder, rain accumulation, precipitation type, thunderstorms and lightning | Radar reflectivity in dBZ or rain rate in mm/h, plus coverage. Capture precipitation type and trailing 3 h and 24 h amounts in mm liquid-water equivalent, including snowfall and convective components where available. Use complete pre-acquisition windows and avoid summing overlapping intervals or adding components to the total. Keep observed lightning count or distance distinguishable from forecast lightning density. |
| Worth checking | Air temperature, dew point, humidity, solar irradiance | Air temperature and dew point at 2 m in K, relative humidity in %, derived vapor-pressure deficit in kPa, and downwelling shortwave irradiance in W/m². Preserve reported heights and add vertical profiles when available. Test surface heating with local land cover and background temperature. |
| Worth checking | Wind and gusts | Eastward and northward wind components at 10 m above ground and gust speed in m/s. Where supported, retain winds at selected pressure levels such as 925 and 850 hPa above the terrain. Test plume movement and changes in the fire between passes. |
| Worth checking | Snow cover and depth | Snow depth in cm and snow-covered fraction where available. Snow changes the surface background and can complicate cloud classification. |
| Worth checking | Aerosol optical depth, dust, fine particles, carbon monoxide | Aerosol optical depth at 550 nm, dust mass, PM₂.₅, PM₁₀, and CO with units and vertical support. These help identify smoke or dust cases. Treat them as proxies for the fire-channel viewing path. Flag modeled smoke based on satellite-derived fire emissions and check results with those fields excluded. [Fire-emissions lineage](https://ads.atmosphere.copernicus.eu/datasets/cams-global-fire-emissions-gfas?tab=overview) |
| Worth checking | Soil moisture, moisture anomaly, drought intensity | For available depth ranges such as 0–40 and 0–100 cm, retain plant-available water as % of capacity, anomaly in mm with reference period, and drought class. Store volumetric soil moisture separately in m³/m³ when available. |
| Worth checking | Fire danger or spread potential, dead fuel moisture | Keep the source danger class and class definitions, continuous fire weather index if available, and dead fuel moisture in % for 10 h, 100 h, and 1,000 h fuel classes. Distinguish the lowest danger class from missing data. These fields describe potential fire behavior. |
| Unlikely | Maximum gust over a forecast window, new snow, wet-bulb temperature, freezing altitude, mean sea-level pressure, CAPE, convective condensation level, icing, clear-air turbulence | Consider in targeted cases where these quantities add information beyond the cloud, precipitation, wind, and humidity fields. For past conditions, use a gust window ending at acquisition. True surface pressure is worth retaining for atmospheric transmission estimates. |
| Unlikely | UV index, air quality index, nitrogen dioxide, column sulfur dioxide, surface and column ozone, extreme temperature/wind/rain indices, weather-event codes, tropical cyclone tracks | Optional case-study fields. Column SO₂ can provide context for volcanic anomalies. Direct weather and cloud measurements carry more information about the observation than regional warnings or composite indices. |
| Unlikely | City heatmaps, sea-surface temperature, waves, wind waves and swells | Use quantitative skin temperature and built-up fraction for urban sites. Sea-surface temperature, wave height, period, and direction are candidates for coastal and offshore glint cases. |
| Absolutely not | Ocean and tidal currents, wave power, warning polygons, avalanche danger, rendered topographic maps, pollen | Omit from the general land-fire comparison. Sample terrain attributes from an elevation model. |
| Absolutely not as detection predictors | Active-fire overlays and satellite-derived fire-intensity layers | Treat detections and FRP as outcomes or comparison data. Using a layer derived from the same detections would give the model access to its own outcome. |

## Hotspot fields and controls

| Priority | Field group | Values to retain or add |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Worth checking | FIRMS identity and thermal measurements | Coordinates, acquisition UTC, platform, instrument, source product and version, day/night, scan and track in km, both native brightness temperatures and their difference in K, raw confidence, and FRP in MW. The [FIRMS parser](../backend/NasaSpaceApps.Api/Firms/FirmsCsvParser.cs) already reads the raw spot fields. Keep the MODIS confidence number and VIIRS confidence class as reported. |
| Worth checking | Footprint and viewing geometry | Native footprint and area where available, view zenith and azimuth, solar zenith and azimuth, and solar glint angle. Keep the measurement source and time of the geometry estimate. Reuse the existing [deferred hotspot geometry](architecture-and-harmonization.md#deferred-hotspot-geometry). |
| Worth checking | Surface and local background | Footprint and neighboring land-cover fractions, class coverage, dataset year and resolution, canopy fraction and height, vegetation greenness, peat/wetland and water fractions, coast distance, elevation, slope and aspect, background skin temperature, emissivity and albedo. The existing [land-cover enrichment](architecture-and-harmonization.md#hotspot-land-cover) samples a fixed 2019 reference at 300 m regionally and 1.2 km globally using an approximate footprint. Keep that sampling information with the class shares. |
| Worth checking where available | Atmospheric transmission and native swath QA | Cloud-mask confidence or fraction, optical thickness, column water vapor, cloud liquid/ice water, surface pressure and vertical profiles. Native fire products can also provide granule and pixel IDs, background temperatures and variability, valid background and adjacent cloud/water counts, saturation and aggregation flags, and FRP retrieval quality. VIIRS can allocate fractions of one 750 m FRP retrieval to several 375 m detections. [MODIS fire product guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) · [VIIRS fire product guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) |
| Worth checking where available | Thermal anomaly type | Inferred vegetation fire, volcano, static land source, or offshore detection. Keep the source flag and allow missing type in the live FIRMS feed. [FIRMS VIIRS field definitions](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/content/descriptions/FIRMS_VIIRS_Firehotspots.html) |
| Unlikely | Moon geometry, background night lights | Use in targeted nighttime, urban, or persistent-source comparisons. |
| Absolutely not as detection predictors | Display radius, marker styling, normalized confidence as a probability, same-sensor FRP for the detection target, or weather valid after acquisition | Preserve physical footprint area and original confidence. Use reported FRP in its own cross-sensor comparison. |

## Joining weather to the live feed

- Use each detection's acquisition time and location. For sufficiently fine grids and imagery, sample across the footprint and record coverage. For coarse model fields, sample the point and retain the native grid resolution. Share samples across nearby spots that use the same model cells and frames.
- For each available value, retain source and model, observation/analysis/forecast status, run time where applicable, valid time or interval, difference from spot acquisition, unit, grid resolution, height or depth reference where applicable, and sampling method. Match continuous fields within the source's supported cadence. Use categorical sampling for codes and explicit bounds for accumulations.
- Set unavailable values to `null` with a reason such as `unsupported`, `outside_coverage`, or `no_matching_frame`. Preserve valid zero values. A rolling 24-hour spot window can include acquisitions older than the weather frames still served by a live source. If the UI shows current weather for those spots, give that weather its own timestamp.
- Keep the enrichment optional. A research response can carry linked weather values keyed by spot, variable, model, level, and time window. This lets analysts test combinations without expanding the backend response for each combination. Verify that the chosen weather source permits the intended use and redistribution before exposing its sampled values in the public API.

## Comparing recent MODIS and VIIRS detections

- Pair detections by footprint overlap and acquisition time. Retain the time difference, overlap fraction, and one-to-many links where several VIIRS pixels overlap one MODIS pixel. Join weather separately at each pass and evaluate detection agreement and FRP differences as separate outcomes.
- Start with sensor interactions involving view angle and footprint area, cloud and view angle, column water vapor and view angle, solar glint and water fraction, and background thermal contrast and land-cover mix. Compare results at progressively wider pairing windows to see how time between passes affects agreement.
- A live feed of positive FIRMS points supports near-coincident agreement among reported detections. A missing point is ambiguous. Estimating missed detections requires actual swath coverage, usable Level 2 pixels, and independent evidence that a fire was present at acquisition. [FIRMS detection limitations](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/alerts/notes.php)
