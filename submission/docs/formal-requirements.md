# Space Apps 2026: formal requirements and checklist

Based on the official guides as retrieved on 14 November 2026 and re-checked against those copies on 15 November. This document contains the official rules only and says nothing about our own project; the status of Earth Fire Calendar against them is in the [requirements status](../working/requirements-status.md). It covers the rules common to the whole submission (team, deadlines, language, access, rights, people, brand, AI). What goes in each form field, and the demo limits, are in the [form fields](../working/form-fields.md).

**⛔ — without this the project will not be judged, will not be accepted or will not be eligible for global judging:** the guides or the participant terms say so explicitly. For items without the mark, the sources name no separate consequence, but they remain requirements. For materials without source references, NASA may remove them and withdraw a nomination, award or winner status.

The 2026 guides call AI **Super Intelligence (SI)**; this document says "AI".

## Checklist

### Team and challenge

- [ ] ⛔ All members, **including the Team Owner**, are listed on the **Members** tab, confirmed at the same Local Event or Universal Event, and not on a waitlist. The team's event is the one where the owner is registered. The guide advises a waitlisted member to contact the organizer (Local Lead); if the organizer refuses or does not reply, the whole team moves to another event with space or to the Universal Event.
- [ ] ⛔ Team size: **1–6 people for a Local Event** or **2–6 for global judging through the Universal Event**.
- [ ] ⛔ The project addresses one of the official Space Apps challenges.
- [ ] Each member is on only one team, and the team submits one project. The challenge on the team page is chosen, and can be changed, by the owner (**Edit Details**).
- [ ] The owner has filled in the required **Team Contact Information** field on the **About** tab: a way to contact the team, visible only to its members.
- [ ] The roster is final: members can be added until the end of the hackathon, 15 November 2026, 23:59 local time. Only the current owner can appoint a new owner. Deleting the team erases everything entered on the Project and Details tabs.
- [ ] Work on the project began no earlier than the start of the hackathon — 14 November 2026, 9:00 local time.
- [ ] Every member registered before the start and takes part as a private individual under their own name. The sources differ here: the participant terms require registration before the start time, while the FAQs say registration stays open through the end of the hackathon on 15 November. If a member registered after the start, confirm with the organizer.
- [ ] No member is a US federal employee: they are barred from registering. Employees of US federal contractors take part voluntarily, outside working hours, without payment under the contract, and do not mention that professional affiliation.
- [ ] Members under 18 were registered by a parent or guardian, who accompanies them throughout the event. Their photographs appear neither in an avatar nor on the team page.
- [ ] ⛔ There is no prohibited activity or affiliation with China or Chinese organizations. The definition covers government bodies of China, including Hong Kong and Macao, China-owned companies, and companies, universities and similar institutions incorporated under Chinese law. NASA decides at its own discretion on the basis of the information the team provided. The restriction concerns ties and activity, **not citizenship**.
- [ ] The time and format of local judging have been confirmed with the organizer: they may differ from the global deadlines.

### Deadline and submission

Submissions opened on 14 November 2026 at 9:00 in the Local Event's time zone; the **Project** tab appeared on the team page at the same time.

- [ ] ⛔ All [required fields](../working/form-fields.md#summary) are filled in and [both checkboxes](../working/form-fields.md#9-two-checkboxes) are ticked.
- [ ] ⛔ The project is [submitted](../working/form-fields.md#form-buttons) **by 23:59 on 15 November 2026** in the Local Event's time zone. For the Universal Event — in the local time of the Team Owner's place of residence.
- [ ] ⛔ After the deadline the submission is neither saved nor resubmitted: a project saved or submitted later is not eligible for judging. According to the official video, requests for edits and exceptions are not accepted.

### Language

- [ ] ⛔ The submission page and all related materials are **in English**.
- [ ] ⛔ Slides are in English only.
- [ ] ⛔ Speech in another language is allowed in the demo video, but then **English subtitles are mandatory**.

### Link access

- [ ] ⛔ Every link opens without sign-in, password, registration or an access request. If the judges cannot open the project, it will not be judged.
- [ ] The links have been checked in a private browser window or by team members.
- [ ] The demo, the final project and everything they depend on stay public and unchanged until the end of judging; the dates are in [After submission](#after-submission). The guides do not say when judges open the links, so this is our reading of the access rule, not a quotation.
- [ ] Optional; the guide recommends it: for a new site, a request for [classification in the FortiGuard web filter](https://www.fortiguard.com/faq/wfratingsubmit) has been filed so that judges' networks block it less often. The company name field takes `N/A`. The team chooses the category itself; the guide gives examples: education, personal website and blogs, reference, web-based application. The decision rests with FortiGuard.

### Demo and NASA data

- [ ] ⛔ There is a demo and it fits within the [limit](../working/form-fields.md#3-link-to-project-demo).
- [ ] The [NASA Data](../working/form-fields.md#7-nasa-data) field is filled in.

### Rights, sources and licenses

- [ ] All external materials are [listed in field 8](../working/form-fields.md#8-space-agency-partner-data-and-other-references).
- [ ] ⛔ The licenses of all third-party materials are named in the documentation submitted with the project. The participant terms say explicitly: without this information NASA will not accept or publish the solution.
- [ ] Third-party materials that do not originate from NASA or partner space agencies are freely available without restriction, or are distributed under an open-source license as defined by the [OSI definition](https://opensource.org/osd).
- [ ] ⛔ The team holds the rights to everything it submits: solutions to which it has no copyright or other rights are not accepted. Copyrighted music, images and text are not used without permission — neither in the project nor in the demo.
- [ ] Images from NASA websites have been checked separately: NASA also hosts third-party copyrighted material, and that hosting does not transfer rights to the team.
- [ ] The original work is distributed freely or under a license named by the team that meets the OSI definition. If no license is named, NASA distributes the work under [Apache 2.0](https://opensource.org/license/apache-2-0).

What the team gives by submitting the project: NASA receives the right to display, copy and distribute the solution; published solutions are available for others to download and use. Submission means consent to the use of the name, place of residence, photograph and information about the solution for informational purposes. The US Government receives a perpetual, royalty-free, non-exclusive license to the submitted video.

### People and personal data

- [ ] ⛔ The demo and the final project contain no images, video, audio or other recognizable likenesses of people under 18: the guide names this as a condition for the project being judged. The participant terms also do not allow recognizable names of minors.
- [ ] **Written** consent to such use has been obtained from every person shown in the video; the team keeps the confirmation and presents it to NASA on request.
- [ ] ⛔ The submission contains no personal data: dates of birth, social security numbers, home addresses, home telephone numbers.

### Prohibited content

Under the participant terms, solutions with such content are not accepted or published.

- [ ] ⛔ No advertising of commercial products and services.
- [ ] ⛔ No proprietary or commercially sensitive information that the team is not entitled to publish.
- [ ] ⛔ No threats, defamation, obscenity, personal attacks, discriminatory statements, sexually explicit or unlawful material.
- [ ] No export-controlled information or other content prohibited by the [participant terms](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/legal/).

### NASA brand

- [ ] The NASA Insignia, Logotype and Seal are not used: the [brand guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/brand-guidelines/) allow them only with explicit permission.
- [ ] Things that are easily mistaken for permitted are not used either: the [NASA Data Insignia](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-data-insignia/) is prohibited without prior approval, and the emblems of missions, projects and programs are not in the public domain.
- [ ] There is no lettering in a font or style resembling the NASA Insignia or Logotype.
- [ ] Factual references to NASA data are kept, and nothing creates an impression of NASA endorsement.

### AI use

What counts as AI and what to answer in the form is in [field 6](../working/form-fields.md#6-use-of-super-intelligence-si). The guide names no separate automatic penalty for breaking the AI rules.

- [ ] The project itself states where and how AI was used.
- [ ] Generated images and video carry a **visible watermark**.
- [ ] For generated audio, code and data, the use of AI is stated in the description **and in the metadata**.
- [ ] Generated material does not use, contain or modify NASA branding and design elements: logos, flags, mission emblems. The NASA Insignia does not appear next to a generated image.
- [ ] If the project itself produces AI answers or materials based on NASA data: the result is attributed to the AI product, not to NASA; there are no phrasings such as "according to NASA"; it is stated that NASA is not responsible for accuracy; mentioning the NASA source material does not imply NASA's review or permission.
- [ ] The idea of the solution belongs to the team: the guide states that AI should not be used to come up with it.

Judges see the AI disclosures in the project and use them to assess originality, concept and execution.

## After submission

- A certificate goes to teams that submitted a project before the deadline. It appears in each member's profile as a PDF; the name is taken from the Full Name field; when downloading, the guide asks you to select the Letter paper size.
- **Global Nominees** will be announced on 3 December 2026, **Global Finalists and Honorable Mentions** on 17 December 2026, and **Global Winners** on 26 January 2027; the winners are celebrated at a virtual ceremony.
- Honorable Mentions do not proceed to final judging (Executive Judging) and cannot receive a global award.
- The first round is local: Global Nominees are chosen by the judges of the Local Event or the Universal Event. Only officially nominated projects reach global judging, so meeting the requirements does not guarantee advancement.
- Universal Event projects are compared only with each other; the number advancing is proportional to the number of submissions.
- Local awards are not tied to global judging; according to the official video, they do not grant automatic advancement.

## Where to ask

The official channel is Space Apps Connect: it has a **Project Submission Help** section for submission questions, and NASA Space Apps Navigators and NASA experts answer questions about the challenge and the data. The platform closes when the hackathon ends; taking part in it is optional.
